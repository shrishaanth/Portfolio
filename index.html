Multi-tenant Kanban where every board edit syncs live across clients, with tenant isolation enforced at the database on every request.

Built to learn multi-tenancy the hard way. SurgeMap

2025

Graph neural network forecasting taxi demand across 253 NYC zones — cuts RMSE by 3–10% against ridge regression, with the largest gains at the longest horizons.

A test of whether graph structure actually helps short-horizon forecasting. It does, and the gap over the linear baseline is widest at 60 minutes out — far enough ahead that a zone’s own recent trips stop being predictive on their own. The convolution is custom, so message-passing follows the direction of the TLC trip graph.

“Neighbours matter most exactly where a zone’s own history matters least.”
01

Graph engine over a 253-zone NYC TLC directed graph with a custom graph convolution.

02

A GRU reads 4-hour spatial history to emit 5 / 15 / 30 / 60-minute forecasts per zone.

03

Benchmarked against ridge regression across every horizon.

Syntropy

2024

Point-in-time quantitative pipeline with walk-forward backtesting and zero future-data leakage — the failure mode that invalidates most student quant work.

Most student quant projects look good because they quietly use data from the future. This one is built around not doing that: every input is as-of its own timestamp, risk is estimated with an EWMA that only ever looks back, and the backtest walks forward one step at a time. The allocator is Hierarchical Risk Parity; the Streamlit dashboard exists so the results can be checked against plain baselines.

“If the backtest is allowed to see the future, the numbers mean nothing.”

DENIED · walk-forward only · no lookahead, ever.

01

Estimates time-varying risk via EWMA on a strict point-in-time basis.

02

Allocates capital through a Hierarchical Risk Parity engine.

03

Outputs a Streamlit dashboard for benchmarking against baselines.
